Welcome to Bureau of Immigration. 
The authentic gateway to the latest travel regulations, visa policies, and immigration 
services. 
Established in 1971, the Bureau of Immigration (BoI) operates under the leadership 
of Commissioner of Immigration. BoI is the primary authority responsible for 
enforcing the latest rules and regulations governing the entry, stay and exit of foreign 
nationals in India. Through the network of FRROs and CHIOs, BoI manages 
immigration facilities across India’s International Check Posts (ICPs). 
BoI ensures smooth, secure and efficient immigration process for travelers arriving in 
or departing from India. BoI also oversees registration services and visa- related 
formalities for foreign nationals residing in India in accordance with relevant Acts, 
Rules and Regulations

Acts, Rules & Orders 
Acts, Rules, Orders & Notifications regarding Foreigners 
All foreign nationals in India & all persons travelling from/ to India are governed by 
the provisions contained in the below mentioned Acts, Rules and Orders made there 
under.
---
The Immigration & Foreigners Act 2025: The Act regulates immigration, entry, and 
stay of foreigners in India. It also regulates Registration of Foreigners in India, 
Liability of Carriers, and Obligations of keeper of accommodation and others to 
furnish particulars. 
 It repeals the following Acts: (i) The Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920, (ii) the 
Registration of Foreigners Act, 1939, (iii) The Foreigners Act 1946, and (iv) The 
Immigration (Carriers’ Liability) Act, 2000.

Immigration & Foreigners Rules 2025: The rules made under Immigration and 
Foreigners Act 2025, consolidate and modernize the regulations governing entry, 
stay and exit of foreign nationals in India.
---
Immigration & Foreigners Order 2025: The Order made in exercise of the powers 
conferred by the Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 and in supersession of the 
Foreigners Order, 1948, the Foreigners (Protected Areas) Order, 1958, the 
Foreigners (Restricted Areas) Order, 1963 and the Foreigners (Tribunals) Order, 
1964. 
 The Order regulates entry into, stay in, movement within and exit from India. 
Further, it regulates restriction of certain activities of foreigners, prevention, 
detention, restricting movements and deportation of Illegal migrants. It also deals 
with Protected & Restricted Areas, Foreigners Tribunals and Prohibited Places.

The Citizenship Act, 1955: It regulates acquisition and termination of Indian 
citizenship as well as Overseas Citizens of India.

Passports Act, 1967: The Act guides on the issue of passports and travel 
documents, to regulate the departure of Citizens of India and other persons, and for 
matters incidental or ancillary thereto.
---
Further Acts, Rules and Regulations pertaining to Foreigners may be referred at the 
website of Foreigners Division, Ministry of Home Affairs (www.mha.gov.in). 
Immigration Fraud 
Immigration fraud is an offence under the Immigration & Foreigners Act, 2025. We 
have wide-ranging measures to detect and prevent immigration offences.

Immigration fraud includes but not limited to the following offences:
---
Using a false or altered document to support a visa application, for example 
Passport, Identity documents etc. 
Providing false or misleading information to support a visa application. 
Helping someone to come to or stay in India who you know either does not have a 
Visa or does not have the appropriate Visa. 
Helping someone to come to India by deceiving them on the purpose of their stay in 
India or your reasons for bringing them to India. 
Using forged or fraudulently obtained travel documents to depart the country or seek 
entry. 
The Government of India has designated 28 Immigration Check Posts (ICPs) across 
the country, each under the jurisdiction of a respective Foreign Regional Registration 
Office (FRRO). 
Ahmedabad in Gujarat is under FRRO Ahmedabad, while Lucknow and Varanasi, 
both in Uttar Pradesh, are controlled by FRRO Lucknow. Amritsar in Punjab falls 
under FRRO Amritsar, which also oversees Chandigarh in Haryana. Bagdogra in 
West Bengal, along with Gaya in Bihar and Guwahati in Assam, are all administered 
by FRRO Kolkata, which additionally governs Kolkata itself. 
Bangalore in Karnataka is under FRRO Bangalore, which also controls Mangalore. 
In Kerala, Calicut and Kannur are under FRRO Calicut, Cochin has its own FRRO, 
and Trivandrum is governed by FRRO Trivandrum. Chennai in Tamil Nadu is under 
FRRO Chennai, which also oversees Coimbatore and Trichy. 
Delhi, being the National Capital Territory, is under FRRO Delhi, which additionally 
controls Jaipur in Rajasthan. Hyderabad in Telangana is governed by FRRO 
Hyderabad. Mumbai in Maharashtra falls under FRRO Mumbai, which has the widest 
jurisdiction — also covering Dabolim and Manohar International Airport, Mopa in 
Goa, Indore and Raja Bhoj International Airport in Bhopal (both in Madhya Pradesh), 
as well as Nagpur and Pune in Maharashtra.
---
The Government of India has designated 21 Immigration Check Posts (ICPs) at 
various seaports and waterways across the country, each under the jurisdiction of a 
respective Foreign Regional Registration Office (FRRO). 
In Kerala, Calicut is under FRRO Calicut; Cochin governs both Cochin port and 
Vallarpadam; while FRRO Trivandrum oversees Kovalam-Vizhinjam, Kollam, and the 
newly developed Vizhinjam International Seaport. Chennai in Tamil Nadu is under 
FRRO Chennai, which also administers Kattupalli. FRRO Mumbai holds the broadest 
jurisdiction among seaports, covering Mumbai, Mormugao Harbour in Goa, and 
Nhava Sheva / Jawaharlal Nehru Port in Maharashtra. New Mangalore in Karnataka 
falls under FRRO Bangalore, and Dhamra in Odisha is governed by FRRO 
Hyderabad. In Gujarat, both Kandla and Mundra are under FRRO Ahmedabad. 
However, the controlling authority has not yet been specified for five ICPs — 
Karimganj, Pandu, Dhubri, and Silghat, all in Assam, as well as Kamarajar in Tamil 
Nadu — indicating that their jurisdictional assignments may be pending or yet to be 
notified.
---
The Government of India has designated 12 Immigration Check Posts (ICPs) at 
various land border crossings across the country, each under the jurisdiction of a 
respective Foreign Regional Registration Office (FRRO). 
In Punjab, Attari Road and Dera Baba Nanak (Kartarpur) are both administered by 
FRRO Amritsar. FRRO Kolkata holds the widest jurisdiction among land border 
ICPs, overseeing Agartala in Tripura, Haridaspur, Jaigoan, Gede, and Ghojadanga

The Government of India has designated 6 Immigration Check Posts (ICPs) at 
various rail and road border crossings, each under the jurisdiction of a respective

FRRO Kolkata holds the widest jurisdiction, overseeing four ICPs — 
Petrapole/Chitpur, Gede Rail and Road Check Post, Haridaspur, and New Jalpaiguri 
Railway Station, all located in West Bengal. Attari in Amritsar, Punjab, is 
administered by FRRO Amritsar. Munabao in Rajasthan falls under the jurisdiction of 
FRRO Delhi.

Health Regulation 
Any person arriving from or Transiting from a Yellow fever endemic area/country by 
Air/Sea has to be in possession of a valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate as 
per the prescribed norms laid down by WHO.

Foreigners or Indians (excluding infants below six months) arriving by air or sea 
without a Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate will be kept in quarantine for a period 
up to 6 daysif they have visited or transited from a Yellow Fever endemic area.

There is no health check requirement for leaving India to Yellow Fever endemic 
countries, however passengers are advised in their own interest to get themselves 
vaccinated and to be in possession of valid yellow fever vaccination certificates 
before they leave the country.

Generally Indian Missions issues visas to Foreign nationals applying from Yellow 
fever endemic countries only after the production of a valid vaccination certificate of 
Yellow Fever and an endorsement “Valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate 
Checked” is made on the visa of the foreigner.

Identity Information 
The Immigration and Foreigners Order 2025, regulates the capturing of biometric 
information as mentioned below:

(1) Every foreigner applying for any category of visa, including registration as 
Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder under section 7A of the Citizenship Act, 1955 
(57 of 1955), shall allow his biometric information to be taken by the authority issuing 
the visa or the authority granting the registration as Overseas Citizen of India 
Cardholder, as the case may be, before grant of such visa or registration as 
Overseas Citizen of India cardholder.
---
(2) A person who is identified as a foreigner or considered to be a foreigner while in 
India shall allow his biometric information to be taken at such time and at such place 
by: -

(a) a specified authority of the State Government or a Union territory Administration;

on the designated electronic portal of the Central Government for the purpose.

(3) The biometric information shall be taken as per specifications specified by the 
Central Government from time to time.

(4) The biometric information captured shall be valid for ten years from the date of 
capturing.

(5) The foreigners who do not have fingerprints or poor fingerprints shall be 
exempted and only facial traits or iris shall be captured for them.

(6) The foreigner who is having less number of fingers shall give fingerprints of 
existing fingers only.

For more details kindly visit Ministry of Home Affairs website at www.mha.gov.in 
Obligation of Carriers 
Obligation of carrier to provide certain information:
---
(1) The country representative or station manager of an airline departing or 
embarking from India to an airport outside India, arriving or landing in India from an 
airport outside India, shall provide the following information to the civil authority or 
the Immigration Officer, namely:

(a) The passenger and crew manifest in Form V;

(b) Advance passenger information data of passengers on board and crew, within 
fifteen minutes of leaving or wheels-off of the aircraft from the port of embarkation 
outside India, and after closure of boarding or wheels-off the aircraft from the port of 
embarkation within India, as per International Civil Aviation Organisation Annex-9 
(Facilitation) data fields and data format UNEDIFACT-PAXLST including passenger 
information (Name, Gender, Date of Birth and Nationality) and travel document 
information of passport and visa (type, number, date of issue and expiry), flight and 
journey information;
---
(c) Passenger name record data of passengers for arrival in India or for departure 
from India at T-72 hours, at T-24 hours, at T-08 hours and at T-0 hours, where T is 
the scheduled time of arrival in India or departure from India, as the case may be, as 
per International Civil Aviation Organisation Annex-9 (Facilitation) data fields and 
data format UNEDIFACT-PNRGOV including passenger information (Name, Gender, 
Date of Birth and Nationality), travel document information of passport and visa 
(type, number, date of issue and expiry), travel and payment information, flight and 
journey information.

(2) The country representative or master of a vessel (cargo or cruise or general 
movement chartered ships) departing or embarking from India to a port or place 
outside India and arriving or berthing in India from a port or place outside India shall 
provide the passenger manifest and crew manifest as in Form VI at T-72 hours, 
where T is the scheduled time of arrival in India or departure from India, as the case 
may be, to the civil authority or the Immigration Officer.
---
Liability of carrier to furnish information: Every carrier shall, when required, provide 
information to the District Magistrate or Commissioner of Police or, where there is no 
Commissioner of Police, Superintendent of Police or the civil authority or the 
Immigration Officer, of passenger and crew manifest in Form V or Form VI, as the 
case may be.

General Declaration furnished by carrier to Immigration Officer:

(1) Every carrier shall submit a general declaration to the Immigration Officer in Form 
VIII prior to the departure of the flight, vessel or other mode of transport at D-45 
minutes, where D is the scheduled departure time.

(2) No flight or vessel or other mode of transport shall be permitted by the carrier to 
depart from a port of place in India until the Immigration Officer has given clearance 
on such general declaration.

Renunciation/Surrendering of Indian Citizenship 
Indian Citizenship Act does not provide for dual citizenship. As per the Passport Act, 
it is mandatory for all Indian passport holders to surrender their last Indian passport 
to the Consulate or Regional Passport office (RPO) in India immediately after 
acquisition of foreign nationality.

It is an offence under the Citizenship Act and the Passports Act for foreign nationals 
to hold Indian passports. Once Indian passport is surrendered, Consulate/ RPO will 
cancel and return it to the Passport holder along with Renunciation/Surrender 
certificate.

For information on surrendering of Indian Passport please visit 
www.passportindia.gov.in

Those who have acquired foreign nationality after 01.06.2010 may apply for 
Renunciation Certificate online on the Ministry of Home Affairs (Foreigners Division) 
website

https://indiancitizenshiponline.nic.in/Renounce
---
Note: On surrender of Indian Passport, Indian Voter ID Card/Election Photo ID Card 
(EPIC Card) needs to be surrendered and in case of Ration Card the record needs 
to be removed by contacting the concerned authority.

OVERSEAS CITIZEN OF INDIA (OCI) CARDHOLDER 
The Government of India had introduced the Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) Card 
and Person of Indian Origin (PIO) Card Scheme to benefit the Indian Diasporas in 
terms of connectivity with the motherland, hassle free travel to India, friendly 
investment regime and benefits in social, economic and educational fields.

Who is an Overseas Citizen of India Cardholder?

A person is said to be an “Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) Cardholder” if he/she is 
registered by the Government of India, under Section 7A of The Citizenship 
(Amendment) Act, 2015.

Eligibility for registration as an OCI Cardholder

Following categories of foreign nationals (except few categories) are eligible for 
registration as Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) Cardholder
---
Any person of full age and capacity: 
Who is a citizen of another country, but was a citizen of India at the time of, or at any 
time after 26.01.1950; or 
Who is a citizen of another country, but was eligible to become a citizen of India as 
on 26.01.1950; or 
Who is a citizen of another country, but belonged to a territory that became part of 
India after 15.08.1947; or 
Who is a child or a grandchild or a great grandchild of such a citizen; or 
A person, who is a minor child of a person mentioned above; or 
A person, who is a minor child, and whose both parents are citizens of India or one 
of the parents is a citizen of India; or 
Spouse of foreign origin of a citizen of India or spouse of foreign origin of an OCI 
cardholder, whose marriage has been registered and subsisted for a continuous 
period of not less than two years immediately preceding the presentation of the 
application. 
Children born out of live-in relationship and IVF/Surrogacy are eligible for registration 
on case-to-case basis subject to merits of the case. 
Who are not eligible for registration as an OCI cardholder?
---
No person, who or either of whose parents or grandparents or great grandparents is 
or had been a citizen of Pakistan, Bangladesh or such other country as notified by 
Government of India, shall be eligible for registration as OCI cardholder. 
Foreign Military/Police Personnel (except Israelis) either serving or retired will not be 
registered as OCI cardholder. 
Foreign nationals who are undergoing compulsory military/police conscription at the 
time of application will not be eligible for registration during the period of such 
conscription. 
Foreign nationals, who are applying for OCI card on spouse basis, shall not be 
eligible if they have undergone/undergoing any military/police conscription. 
A foreign national holding Diplomatic Passport will not be registered as OCI 
cardholder. 
Merger of Persons of Indian Origin (PIO) Scheme with OCI
---
The erstwhile Person of Indian Origin (PIO) card scheme has been rescinded by the 
Government of India and all the existing PIO cardholders shall be deemed to be 
Overseas Indian Citizens of India (OCI) cardholders. However, all persons who are 
still holding PIO cards are advised to apply for conversion of their PIO cards into OCI 
card through OCI Miscellaneous Services.
---
If the foreigner gets remarried to another foreigner/Indian, he/she should surrender 
the existing OCI card to the concerned FRRO/Indian Mission. 
If it is found that the registration as an OCI Cardholder was obtained by means of 
fraud, false representation or concealment of any material fact or the registered OCI 
Cardholder has shown disaffection towards the Constitution of India or contravened 
any provisions of the Citizenship Act, 1955, the registration of such person will be 
cancelled forthwith. 
Please visit the website https://ociservices.gov.in or visit the nearest Indian 
Mission/FRRO for more details 
Important Guidelines for OCI Cardholders 
Passport is the primary travel document. OCI is only a life-long visa. (OCI card 
issued to spouse of Indian/ Indian origin on or after 03.09.2025 will have an initial 
validity of 5 years, which is further extendable to lifelong subject to scrutiny)

Stay of foreigners in India without a valid passport is illegal even if he or she is an 
OCI cardholder. Hence, it is necessary that OCI cardholders staying in India should 
be in possession of a valid passport and OCI card at all times of his/her stay in the 
country .

Foreigners registered as OCI Cardholders and staying in India are exempted from 
the requirement of Registration with jurisdictional FRROs.

The ‘U’ (Universal) visa sticker on the foreign passport of OCI cardholders is 
dispensed with.

Foreigners who intend to submit their applications for registration as OCI cardholder 
to a jurisdictional FRRO in India, should be ‘Ordinarily Resident ’, which

In such cases, a copy of any long duration visa (other than Tourist, Missionary, 
Mountaineering Visa) with 3 months validity as on the date of application along with 
Registration Certificate (RC/RP) issued by FRRO/FRO should be submitted along 
with the application mandatorily. 
Capturing of biometrics of fingerprints & face (if technically feasible) in case of 
applicants for registration as OCI cardholders is mandatory. 
In case the Birth/Marriage Certificate is issued by a foreign authority, it is to be 
Apostled or endorsed by the concerned Indian Mission abroad/concerned foreign 
Mission in India. 
An OCI cardholder staying in India, who require a Police Clearance Certificate (PCC) 
shall apply for such certificate to the FRRO/FRO concerned as per their place of

please visit https://indianfrro.gov.in 
OCI Cardholders shall also require Protected Area/Restricted Area Permit 
(RAP/PAP) to visit any place which falls within the Protected/Restricted Area as 
notified by the Government of India. For more info, please check the ‘Entry to 
Restricted/Protected Areas’ page on this website

Registration Requirements 
Foreign Nationals visiting India are required to register with the Foreigner’s Regional 
Registration Office (FRRO) or the Foreigner’s Registration Office (FRO) as per their 
visa category and endorsement. Details are as under:

Foreign nationals entering India on a Student, Employment, Medical or Missionary 
visa where the visa is valid for more than 180 days, should register with the 
jurisdictional FRRO/FRO within 14 days of their arrival in India. 
Business visa holders should register with the jurisdictional FRRO/FRO if their 
aggregate stay in India within the calendar year (Jan to Dec) exceeds 180 days or 
their continuous stay in India exceeds 180 days. 
Foreign Nationals entering India on any type of visa (except on Official/ Diplomatic 
passport) will be required to register, if their aggregate stay in a calendar year or 
continuous stay on each entry exceeds 180 days. 
Pakistani nationals are required to register with their local FRO within 24 hours of 
their arrival in India. 
Note:
---
Children below 12 years of age do not require to register with the FRO/FRRO. 
OCI cardholders are exempt from the requirement of registration. 
Citizens of Nepal and Bhutan are exempt from the requirement of visa and 
registration. 
All specific conditions and stipulations endorsed in the visa shall be adhered to at all 
times. 
Non-compliance with registration rules shall result in legal consequences.

Special Categories 
Pakistan: Every Pakistan national who enter India must register within 24 hours of 
arrival. This condition is exempted for those individuals who have ‘Exemption from 
police reporting’ endorsed on their visas. Also, Pakistan nationals on Medical visa 
should register within 7 days of arrival in India. 
Afghanistan: Afghanistan nationals requesting for 'Stay Visa/ Residential Permit' 
shall apply online on https://indianfrro.gov.in. 
Bangladesh: Bangladesh nationals arriving in India on valid visas, and wishing to 
stay in India for a continuous period exceeding six months, will be required to 
register themselves with the respective FRRO/ FRO and obtain a residential permit 
within 14 days of their arrival. 
Note:
---
Certain categories of Afghanistan/ Bangladesh/ Pakistan nationals staying in India 
are eligible for grant of Long Term Visas (LTV). 
Detailed instructions on grant/ extension of LTV facilities are available on 
https://www.mha.gov.in/PDF_Other/AnnexVI_01022018.pdf. 
These conditions are subject to change from time to time and the latest guidelines 
shall apply.

Visa Extension or Visa Conversion 
Foreign Nationals who are unable to leave/ exit India within the validity of their visa 
due to justifiable reasons, should apply for the appropriate visa service on 
https://indianfrro.gov.in.

Such visa services are available only to foreign nationals who are present in India. 
This request/ application should be made within the validity of their current visa, 
allowing reasonable time for processing. 
If the extension/ conversion of the visa/ permission to continue their stay is not 
granted within the validity of their current visa, they must depart from India. Failure to 
depart will be considered a willful illegal overstay and will be treated in accordance 
with the law.

Exit Permit 
Loss of Passport: If a Foreign national loses his/her travel document (passport), 
he/she should promptly report the loss to the nearest police station and the 
jurisdictional FRRO/FRO. He/she should then approach the respective embassy or 
mission in India and apply for a new travel document. Upon obtaining the new 
document, he/she should apply for the necessary visa service on 
https://indianfrro.gov.in. 
Adoptions: 
Foreign nationals adopting an Indian child under CARA guidelines must obtain exit 
permits: 
If the adopted child retains Indian nationality, then both parents need to take exit 
permits to travel out of India. 
If the adopted child has acquired the nationality of the country to which either of the 
adoptive parent belongs, then both parents and the adopted child need to take exit 
permits to travel out of India. 
Even if the parents are OCI card holders, the above mentioned conditions shall 
apply. 
Foreign Nationals, except OCI card holders, are not permitted to commission 
surrogacy in India. 
Overstaying / Violation of Visa Conditions: Foreigners who are overstaying in India or 
who have violated their visa conditions require Exit Permit to depart from India.

The treatment of the disease requires an organ transplant.

Acquisition of Foreign Nationality by an Indian National: 
India does not recognize the concept of dual nationality. Any Indian national who 
obtains a foreign citizenship ceases to be an Indian national from the day of 
acquisition of the new nationality.

Holding an Indian Passport/ acquiring an Indian Passport/ travelling on Indian 
Passport (excluding the grace period of 3 months after the acquisition of the foreign 
nationality) after acquisition of foreign citizenship (i.e. Naturalization certificate) 
constitutes an offence under the Indian Passport Act 1967 and attracts penalties.

Case 1: Indian national staying in India acquires a foreign nationality:
---
Obtain the passport of the new nationality. 
Surrender the Indian passport, Indian Voter ID, Indian Ration card, and all other 
relevant Indian nationality documents. 
Apply for visa services on https://indianfrro.gov.in/ within 30 days of acquisition of 
foreign nationality. 
Case 2: Indian national staying abroad acquires foreign citizenship but enters India 
on an Indian Passport:

Surrender the Indian passport, Indian Voter ID, Indian Ration card, and all other 
relevant Indian nationality documents. 
Apply for visa services on https://indianfrro.gov.in/ within 30 days of arrival in India.

India is not a signatory to the 1951 United Nations Convention on the Status of 
Refugees and the 1967 Protocol thereof.

A foreign national who holds UNHCR card for Refugees/ Stateless may apply for 
appropriate visa service at https://indianfrro.gov.in. Applications will be evaluated by 
the jurisdictional FRRO.

Tibetan Origin person: 
Tibetans who entered India before 30.05.2003 are eligible for a 'Long Term Stay' 
status and can obtain Registration Certificates from FRROs/FROs. 
Tibetan nationals who arrived under Special Entry Permit (SEP) scheme after 
30.05.2003 must register within 14 days, regardless of their visit's purpose or SEP 
validity. 
Tibetan migrants intending to visit abroad shall travel only on the strength of the 
Certificate of Identity and Return Visa which may be issued by FRRO/ FRO. If the 
intended visit abroad requires absence from India for a period of more than 15 days, 
such Tibetan migrants would be required to take exit permission from the concerned 
FRRO /FRO. 
Tibetans born in India to Tibetan Refugee couples and issued birth certificate by 
Tibetan Welfare Officer/ Tibetan Settlement Officer may be issued registration 
certificate on the basis of such certificate issued till January 01, 2003 clearly 
indicating on RCs that such birth certificates have been accepted specifically for the 
purpose of registration only. 
After January 01, 2003, birth certificate issued by the competent authority under the 
Registration of Births and Deaths Act 1969 would be acceptable for the purpose of 
registration only.

orders made under Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 are subjected to 
penal/legal action as mentioned.Overstay, non-registration, engaging in activities not

contravening any of the provisions or orders made under Immigration and Foreigners 
Act, 2025 are subjected to penal/legal action as mentioned. 
As per various sections under Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025, foreigners as 
well as Indian stake holders like carriers, hoteliers, educational institutions or 
hospitals where foreigner associate with, are liable for legal action, penalties ranging

any of the provisions of said act.

India and/ or Indian Visas in the future.

FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) 
1. What is an ETA? 
Ans: ETA is an Electronic Travel Authorization. It allows the bearer 
access into the issuing country (India). ETA is NOT eVisa. When the 
bearer of ETA is allowed entry into India by the Immigration Officer, it 
becomes eVisa. eVisa will be stamped on the Passport of the foreigner 
at the designated ICPs on production of valid ETA. 
2. What is the difference between “ETA Expiry date” and “Visa Expiry 
Date”? 
Ans: ETA expiry date is the last date allowing bearer access into India. 
Visa Expiry Date is the last date permitted for the stay of foreigner in 
India subjected to any stay stipulation applicable. 
3. What activities can I undertake on my Visa? 
Ans: You are required to strictly adhere to the purpose of visit declared 
while submitting the visa application. Kindly refer to terms and 
conditions of your Visa. 
4. Do I need to register with FRRO (Foreigners Regional Registration 
Officer) after arriving in India? 
Ans: Click here for more details.  
5. What is the difference between “Visa validity/Visa expiry date” and “Stay 
stipulation”? 
Visa validity or Visa expiry date refers to the validity period during which 
the foreigner can stay in India on the strength of Visa subject to stay 
stipulations. 
Stay stipulations are the rules which determine the number of days a 
foreigner may be permitted to stay in India on the strength of Visa. Stay 
stipulations are of two kinds 1) Continuous stay in India from the date of 
last arrival. 2) Total Aggregate stay in India during the calendar year. 
6. What is the maximum stay allowed on Tourist Visa? 
Maximum stay by a foreigner in India on an e-Tourist Visa or paper 
Tourist Visa or both in one calendar year shall be restricted to 180 days. 
7. What is a calendar year? 
Ans: Calendar year refers to period from 1st January to 31st December 
of the current year. 
8. What is “total aggregate stay in a calendar year” and “continuous 
stay”? 
Ans: Total Aggregate stay refers to total period of stay of foreigner in 
India including multiple visits during the calendar year. 
Continuous stay refers to period of stay of foreigner in India since his 
last arrival. 
9. What is maximum duration of stay in Transit if I am not leaving the 
airport premises? 
Ans: Foreigner may be permitted to stay in the designated area of the 
Airport for the purpose of travelling via connecting flight from India for a 
period of 24 hours. Transit Visa should be obtained if the waiting period 
is longer. 
10. In case I reach the Arrival Immigration Counter few hours after the 
expiry of my ETA due to flight delays etc., will I be allowed entry into 
India on the ETA? 
Ans: It may be considered on case to case basis by the Immigration 
Authorities. 
11. In case I reach the Departure Immigration Counter few hours after the 
expiry of my Visa, will I be allowed to depart from India on that Visa? 
Ans: It may be considered on case to case basis by the Immigration 
Authorities. 
12. Can I undertake Tourism while on visit to India on Business/ 
Conference/ Medical Visa? 
Ans: Foreign nationals who are granted a particular type of visa with 
reference to the purpose of visit shall be required to strictly adhere to 
the purpose of visit declared while submitting the visa application. 
However, a foreign national (other than a Pakistani national) coming to 
India on any type of visa will be allowed to avail activities permitted 
under Tourist Visa. 
13. What is the procedure to obtain Political Clearance Letter issued by 
Ministry of External Affairs, as required for e-Conference Visa? 
Ans: Kindly contact Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) for details. 
14. What is the procedure to be followed if child is born in India to parents 
of foreign nationality? 
Ans: Click here for details child born in India

Call Center Scams 
Indian nationals are being lured by travel agents with false promises of lucrative job 
opportunities in marketing and administrative roles at major casinos in certain South 
East Asian countries. These agents offer high salaries and paid accommodation. 
Recent trends show that upon arrival they are tricked into working for call centres 
engaged in crypto related scams, fraud investment schemes and are engaged in 
cyber crime activities in those countries. Hence it is advised to observe due caution 
regarding this trend. 
Employment in ECR countries without obtaining POE 
Indian nationals holding ECR passports travelling to ECR countries on non-work 
Visas without POE certificate are advised to not take up Employment in ECR 
countries as they may be subjected to harsh work environment and are vulnerable to 
exploitation without any legal protection or recourse. Instances of travelling to a third 
country on tourist visa and further migrating to ECR Countries is observed as a trend 
to bypass the POE Certificates which results in exploitation of those ECR Passport 
holders in work environments. 
Illegal migration to Western Europe 
Indian nationals are advised not to be lured by travel agents offering opportunity for 
illegal migration to Western European countries via third country; As They may face 
exploitation, human trafficking and often seen forced to work in harsh work 
environments without any legal recourse. Further, they are also open to prosecution 
from the host country for their illegal entry/stay. Travellers are advised to verify the 
documents with care.

Immigration >> Policy and Law >> Immigration Policies & Process (Acts, Rules & Orders) 
Acts, Rules, Orders & Notifications regarding Foreigners 
All foreign nationals in India & all persons travelling from/ to India are governed by the provisions 
contained in the below mentioned Acts, Rules and Orders made there under. 
1. The Immigration & Foreigners Act 2025: The Act regulates immigration, entry, and stay of 
foreigners in India. It also regulates Registration of Foreigners in India, Liability of Carriers, and 
Obligations of keeper of accommodation and others to furnish particulars. 
 It repeals the following Acts: (i) The Passport (Entry into India) Act, 1920, (ii) the Registration of 
Foreigners Act, 1939, (iii) The Foreigners Act 1946, and (iv) The Immigration (Carriers’ Liability) Act, 
2000.
---
2. Immigration & Foreigners Rules 2025: The rules made under Immigration and Foreigners Act 2025, 
consolidate and modernize the regulations governing entry, stay and exit of foreign nationals in 
India.

3. Immigration & Foreigners Order 2025: The Order made in exercise of the powers conferred by the 
Immigration and Foreigners Act, 2025 and in supersession of the Foreigners Order, 1948, the 
Foreigners (Protected Areas) Order, 1958, the Foreigners (Restricted Areas) Order, 1963 and the 
Foreigners (Tribunals) Order, 1964. 
 The Order regulates entry into, stay in, movement within and exit from India. Further, it regulates 
restriction of certain activities of foreigners, prevention, detention, restricting movements and 
deportation of Illegal migrants. It also deals with Protected & Restricted Areas, Foreigners 
Tribunals and Prohibited Places.

4. The Citizenship Act, 1955:  It regulates acquisition and termination of Indian citizenship as well as 
Overseas Citizens of India.

5. Passports Act, 1967: The Act guides on the issue of passports and travel documents, to regulate 
the departure of Citizens of India and other persons, and for matters incidental or ancillary 
thereto.
---
Further Acts, Rules and Regulations pertaining to Foreigners may be referred at the website of 
Foreigners Division, Ministry of Home Affairs ( www.mha.gov.in).

Immigration Fraud 
Immigration fraud is an offence under the Immigration & Foreigners Act, 2025. We have wide-ranging 
measures to detect and prevent immigration offences. 
Immigration fraud includes but not limited to the following offences: 
• Using a false or altered document to support a visa application, for example Passport, Identity 
documents etc. 
• Providing false or misleading information to support a visa application. 
• Helping someone to come to or stay in India who you know either does not have a Visa or does not 
have the appropriate Visa. 
• Helping someone to come to India by deceiving them on the purpose of their stay in India or your 
reasons for bringing them to India. 
• Using forged or fraudulently obtained travel documents to depart the country or seek entry.

Health Regulation 
• Any person arriving from or Transiting from a Yellow fever endemic area/country by Air/Sea has to 
be in possession of a valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate as per the prescribed norms laid 
down by WHO. 
• Foreigners or Indians (excluding infants below six months) arriving by air or sea without a Yellow 
Fever Vaccination Certificate will be kept in quarantine for a period up to 6 daysif they have 
visited or transited from a Yellow Fever endemic area. 
• There is no health check requirement for leaving India to Yellow Fever endemic countries, however 
passengers are advised in their own interest to get themselves vaccinated and to be in possession 
of valid yellow fever vaccination certificates before they leave the country. 
• Generally Indian Missions issues visas to Foreign nationals applying from Yellow fever endemic 
countries only after the production of a valid vaccination certificate of Yellow Fever and an 
endorsement “Valid Yellow Fever Vaccination Certificate Checked” is made on the visa of the 
foreigner.

Immigration >> Policy and Law >> Obligation of Carriers 
Obligation of carrier to provide certain information: 
(1) The country representative or station manager of an airline departing or embarking from India to an 
airport outside India, arriving or landing in India from an airport outside India, shall provide the 
following information to the civil authority or the Immigration Officer, namely: 
(a) The passenger and crew manifest in Form V; 
(b) Advance passenger information data of passengers on board and crew, within fifteen minutes of 
leaving or wheels-off of the aircraft from the port of embarkation outside India, and after closure of 
boarding or wheels-off the aircraft from the port of embarkation within India, as per International Civil 
Aviation Organisation Annex-9 (Facilitation) data fields and data format UNEDIFACT-PAXLST including 
passenger information (Name, Gender, Date of Birth and Nationality) and travel document 
information of passport and visa (type, number, date of issue and expiry), flight and journey 
information; 
(c) Passenger name record data of passengers for arrival in India or for departure from India at T-72 
hours, at T-24 hours, at T-08 hours and at T-0 hours, where T is the scheduled time of arrival in India or 
departure from India, as the case may be, as per International Civil Aviation Organisation Annex-9 
(Facilitation) data fields and data format UNEDIFACT-PNRGOV including passenger information 
(Name, Gender, Date of Birth and Nationality), travel document information of passport and visa 
(type, number, date of issue and expiry), travel and payment information, flight and journey 
information. 
(2) The country representative or master of a vessel (cargo or cruise or general movement chartered 
ships) departing or embarking from India to a port or place outside India and arriving or berthing in 
India from a port or place outside India shall provide the passenger manifest and crew manifest as in 
Form VI at T-72 hours, where T is the scheduled time of arrival in India or departure from India, as the 
case may be, to the civil authority or the Immigration Officer. 
Liability of carrier to furnish information: Every carrier shall, when required, provide information to 
the District Magistrate or Commissioner of Police or, where there is no Commissioner of Police, 
Superintendent of Police or the civil authority or the Immigration Officer, of passenger and crew 
manifest in Form V or Form VI, as the case may be. 
General Declaration furnished by carrier to Immigration Officer: 
(1) Every carrier shall submit a general declaration to the Immigration Officer in Form VIII prior to the 
departure of the flight, vessel or other mode of transport at D-45 minutes, where D is the scheduled 
departure time. 
(2) No flight or vessel or other mode of transport shall be permitted by the carrier to depart from a port 
of place in India until the Immigration Officer has given clearance on such general declaration.
