OVERSEAS CITIZEN OF INDIA (OCI) Eligibility FAQ
---CHUNK---
[INDIAN CITIZENSHIP]
Q: What is an Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) Cardholder?
A:
An OCI Cardholder is a foreign national who is officially registered by
the Government of India under Section 7A of The Citizenship
(Amendment) Act, 2015. The scheme was created to help the Indian
Diaspora maintain a strong connection with India through benefits
like hassle-free travel and a friendly investment regime.
---CHUNK---
[INDIAN CITIZENSHIP]
Q: Who is generally eligible to apply for an OCI card?
A: Most
foreign nationals of full age and capacity are eligible if they meet one
of the following criteria:
• They were an Indian citizen on or after January 26, 1950.
• They were eligible to become an Indian citizen on January 26,
1950.
• They belonged to a territory that became part of India after
August 15, 1947.
• They are a child, grandchild, or great-grandchild of someone
who meets the above criteria.
---CHUNK---
[INDIAN CITIZENSHIP]
Q: Can minor children apply for an OCI card?
A: Yes. A minor
child is eligible if:
• They are the child of a person eligible for an OCI card.
• Both of their parents are citizens of India, or at least one parent
is a citizen of India.
---CHUNK---
[INDIAN CITIZENSHIP]
Q: I am the spouse of an Indian citizen. Am I eligible?
A: Yes,
spouses of foreign origin are eligible if their marriage to an Indian
citizen or an existing OCI cardholder has been officially registered and
has lasted for a continuous period of at least two years immediately
before the application is submitted.
---CHUNK---
[INDIAN CITIZENSHIP]
Q: What about children born through IVF, surrogacy, or live-

India on any other type of visa (such as a Business or Student visa)
are permitted to engage in activities typically allowed under a Tourist
Visa.
---CHUNK---
[SEAMEN AND MARITIME CREW FAQ]
Q: What counts as a "short-term yoga programme"?
A: A short-term yoga program is defined as one that does not exceed
6 months in duration and does not result in the issuance of a formal
qualifying certificate or diploma.
---CHUNK---
[SEAMEN AND MARITIME CREW FAQ]
Q: Is there a mandatory gap between two visits?
A: For most travelers, there is no longer a gap requirement. However,
a gap of at least 2 months is mandatory between two visits for
nationals of the following countries:
• Afghanistan, China, Iraq, and Sudan.
• Foreigners of Pakistani origin and stateless persons.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Is there a limit on how long I can stay in India on a Tourist
Visa in a single year?
A: Yes. The maximum time a foreigner can stay in India on a Tourist
or e-Tourist Visa is 180 days total within a single calendar year
(January to December). Even if you have a multi-year visa, you must
ensure your combined visits don't cross this 180-day threshold.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Can I extend my Tourist Visa if I want to stay longer?
A: No. Under standard rules, Tourist and e-Tourist Visas are strictly
non-extendable. You must depart the country before your permitted
stay or visa validity expires.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Am I allowed to convert my Tourist Visa into a Student or
Work Visa while I'm in India?
A: Generally, no. Conversion of a Tourist Visa into any other visa type
is not permitted while you are inside the country. If your purpose of
stay changes, you usually need to leave India and apply for the
correct visa category from abroad.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Is there a mandatory gap required between two visits to
India?
A: For most nationalities, there is no longer a mandatory "two-month
gap" between visits. However, a 2-month gap between arrivals is still
strictly required for nationals of Afghanistan, China, Iraq, Sudan, and
Iran, as well as for stateless persons and foreigners of Pakistani
origin.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Can I take a yoga course while on a Tourist Visa?
A: Yes, you can attend a short-term yoga program on a Tourist Visa,
provided the course does not exceed 6 months in duration. Please
note that these should be casual or experiential courses; the Tourist
Visa does not cover structured programs that result in a formal
diploma or qualifying certificate.

---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Which countries are exempt from the Indian visa
requirement?
A: Citizens of Nepal, Bhutan, and the Maldives are eligible for visa-
free entry into India under specific conditions.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Under what conditions can Maldivian citizens enter India
without a visa?
A: Maldivian nationals can avail of visa-free entry exclusively for
Tourism, Medical treatment, or Business purposes, provided their stay
does not exceed 90 days.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: How is the 90-day stay calculated for Maldivians?
A: The 90-day limit is cumulative. It includes all prior stays in India
during the 6 months immediately preceding your current date of
arrival. If you intend to stay longer than 90 days, you must apply for
a Regular Visa.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Can Maldivian seamen use the visa-free entry to "Sign-On"
to a vessel?
A: No. The "Sign-On" facility is not available to seamen who enter
India under the visa-free regime.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: Do citizens of Nepal and Bhutan need a passport to enter
India?
A: If they are visiting India directly from Nepal or Bhutan (by land
or air), they are exempt from holding a passport or visa. However,
they must carry a government-approved photo ID that authenticates
their nationality (such as a Voter ID).
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: When is a passport mandatory for Nepalese or Bhutanese
citizens?
A: A valid passport is required in the following scenarios:
• When entering or exiting India from/to a third country (any
country other than Nepal, Bhutan, or India).
• When entering India specifically from China, Macau, Hong
Kong, or Pakistan. In these cases, a passport and a valid
Indian visa are mandatory.
---CHUNK---
[QUICK COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST FOR TOURISTS FAQ]
Q: What details must the identification document contain?
A: If not using a passport, the alternative document must include:
• Given Name and Last Name.
• Date of Birth and Nationality.
• Photograph.
• Issuing Authority and Period of Validity.
---CHUNK---
[VISA-FREE ENTRY QUICK REFERENCE FAQ]
Q: What are the visa rules for Maldivian citizens visiting India?
A: Citizens of the Maldives can enter India without a visa for tourism,
medical treatment, or business purposes. This stay is limited to 90
days within any 6-month period, and travellers must be in possession
of a valid passport.
---CHUNK---
[VISA-FREE ENTRY QUICK REFERENCE FAQ]
Q: Do citizens of Nepal need a passport to enter India?
A: Not necessarily. If a Nepalese citizen is entering India directly from
Nepal, there is no limit on their stay and they can use either a valid
passport or a government-issued photo ID that proves their
nationality. However, if they are entering India from a third country,
a passport and visa become mandatory.
---CHUNK---
[VISA-FREE ENTRY QUICK REFERENCE FAQ]
Q: What documents are required for Bhutanese citizens
traveling to India?
A: Similar to Nepalese citizens, Bhutanese nationals traveling directly
from Bhutan do not have a stay limit and can enter using a
government-issued photo ID or a passport. If they arrive from a
country other than Bhutan or India, they must have a valid passport
and an Indian visa.
---CHUNK---
[VISA-FREE ENTRY QUICK REFERENCE FAQ]
Q: Are there special rules for Nepalese or Bhutanese citizens
arriving from places like China or Pakistan?
A: Yes. Even though there is a visa-free regime for these citizens, if
they are entering India from China, Macau, Hong Kong, or Pakistan,
they are strictly required to be in possession of both a valid passport
and a visa for India.
---CHUNK---
[VISA-FREE ENTRY QUICK REFERENCE FAQ]
Q: What happens if a Maldivian citizen needs to stay in India
for more than 90 days?
A: The visa-free facility for Maldivians is strictly capped at 90 days
(which includes any time spent in India during the previous 6
months). If the intended visit is longer than 90 days, a regular Indian
visa must be obtained before arrival.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: What type of visa do I need to work in India?
A: Foreigners desiring to work in India must arrive on a valid
Employment Visa. This is typically reserved for highly skilled or
qualified professionals.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Who is eligible for an Employment Visa?
A: You are eligible if you are being engaged or appointed by:
• An Indian company, organization, or industry.
• A foreign firm engaged in the execution of a project in India.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Is there a minimum salary requirement for working in
India?
A: Yes. Foreigners must draw a gross salary exceeding Rs. 16.25
lakhs per annum.
Note: Some specific sectors notified by the Government of India
(GOI) may be exempt from this annual floor limit.
Non-Profit & Volunteer Work
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Do I need a visa for voluntary work or NGOs?
A: Yes. If you intend to work with a Non-Profit or NGO on a voluntary
basis (with or without an honorarium), you must obtain an
Employment (E-3) Visa.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Is there a limit on the stipend for volunteers?
A: Yes, if an honorarium is paid, it is subject to a ceiling of Rs. 10,000
per month.
Business Owners & Promoters
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I own the majority stake in a company; do I need an
Employment Visa?
A: No. If you are a promoter or owner of a company holding majority
stakes/shareholdings, you are required to enter India on a Business
Visa.
Restrictions & Compliance
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I change my employer while in India?
A: No. A change of employer is not permitted during the currency of
your employment visa. To change jobs, you must leave India and
apply for a fresh Employment Visa.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I work for two companies at the same time?
A: No. Foreigners are prohibited from working for more than one
company simultaneously on an Employment Visa.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: What happens if I resign or lose my job?
A: You must inform the FRRO/FRO (Foreigners Regional Registration
Office) concerned immediately if you discontinue your employment.
---CHUNK---
[EMPLOYMENT VISA: FAQ]
Q: What are my legal obligations while working in India?
A: You must comply with all Indian legal requirements, including
meeting the minimum salary threshold and fulfilling all tax liabilities.
Q1: I’m a professional and I've been offered a job in India.
What visa should I apply for?
A: You should apply for an Employment Visa. This visa is specifically
for highly skilled or qualified professionals who have been appointed
by an Indian company or a foreign firm working on a project in India.
Q2: I want to volunteer at an NGO in India. Is there a specific
visa for that?
A: Yes, even for voluntary work, you need an Employment (E-3) Visa.
This applies whether you are working for free or receiving a small
honorarium (which is capped at Rs. 10,000 per month).
Q3: If I own the majority of shares in a company I’m starting
in India, do I still need an Employment Visa?
A: No, in that case, you should enter on a Business Visa. If you are a
promoter or a majority shareholder/owner of a company, the Business
Visa is the correct category for your entry.
Q4: Can I switch to a different company while I’m already in
India on an Employment Visa?
A: Unfortunately, no. You aren't allowed to change employers while
your current visa is active. If you decide to take a job with a different
company, you must leave India and apply for a fresh Employment
Visa under your new employer.
Q5: Is it possible to work for two different Indian companies
at the same time?
A: No, that is not permitted. Under the terms of an Employment Visa,
you are only allowed to work for one company at a time.
Q6: Are there any specific salary requirements I should know
about?
A: Yes, generally you must earn a gross salary of more than Rs. 16.25
lakhs per year to qualify for an Employment Visa. However, the
government does exempt certain sectors from this rule from time to
time, so it's worth checking if your specific industry is on that list.
Q7: What do I need to do if I quit my job or my contract ends?
A: If you discontinue your employment, you (or your employer) must
inform the concerned FRRO/FRO office. Additionally, you are
responsible for ensuring all legal requirements are met before you
leave, such as paying your final tax liabilities.

[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I’m just passing through India to another country. Do I
need a Transit Visa?
A: Yes, if your sole purpose is to travel through India to a destination
outside the country, a Transit Visa is required. However, there are
specific exceptions for air and sea travelers who stay within
designated areas.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: How long is a Transit Visa valid for?
A: Ordinarily, it is valid for a single journey and must be used for
entry within 15 days from the date it was issued. Note that its validity
will never exceed the validity of the visa you hold for your final
destination country.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: How long can I stay in India on a Transit Visa?
A: A Transit Visa is only for "direct transit." This means each visit
cannot exceed 3 days. If you plan to stay in India for more than 72
hours, you must apply for an appropriate visa category (like a Tourist
Visa).
Air & Sea Travel Exceptions
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I have a layover at an Indian airport. Do I need a visa if I
don't leave the airport?
A: You do not need a Transit Visa if you are in direct transit by air and
remain within the specified precincts of the airport for less than 24
hours. If your layover exceeds 24 hours, or if you need to leave the
airport precincts (e.g., to stay at a hotel outside), a Transit Visa is
required.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I’m traveling by ship. Do I need a visa when we dock at an
Indian port?
A: No, a Transit Visa is not required as long as you do not leave the
ship while it is at the port.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I leave my ship to catch a flight from a nearby airport?
A: Yes, but only with prior approval from the competent authority.
You must hold a valid ticket for a scheduled air service and will only
be allowed to leave the ship to proceed directly to the designated
airport.
Important Restrictions & Extensions
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: What happens if I don't travel within the 15-day validity
period?
A: If you do not perform your journey through India within the validity
period of your Transit Visa, the visa will lapse. You will need to apply
and pay for a fresh Transit Visa.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I extend my Transit Visa if my plans change?
A: Generally, no. Transit Visas are not extendable. Extensions are only
granted in extreme emergencies, such as medical illness, inclement
weather, or major strikes/traffic disruptions.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I use a Transit Visa to do a quick 4-day tour?
A: No. If you intend to stay in India for more than 3 days for any
reason, you must obtain a visa of the appropriate category, such as
an e-Tourist Visa.
India Transit Regulations
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I have a layover in India. Do I always need a Transit Visa?
A: Not necessarily. If you are traveling by air and staying inside the
airport's transit area for less than 24 hours, you do not need a visa.
However, if your layover is longer than 24 hours or you plan to leave
the airport, you must have a Transit Visa.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: How long can I actually stay in the country on a Transit
Visa?
A: A Transit Visa is strictly for short stops. You can stay for a
maximum of 3 days (72 hours) per visit. If you plan to be in India for
more than 3 days, you’ll need to apply for a different category, like a
Tourist Visa.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: I am traveling by sea. Do I need a visa when the ship stops
at an Indian port?
A: If you stay on board the ship while it is docked, you do not need a
visa. If you intend to leave the ship to catch a flight, you will need a
visa and prior approval from the competent authorities to proceed to
the airport.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: How long is the Transit Visa valid once it is issued?
A: Generally, a Transit Visa is valid for a single journey and must be
used within 15 days of being issued. Also, keep in mind that its
validity cannot exceed the validity of the visa you hold for your final
destination country.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: My flight was delayed due to bad weather. Can I extend my
Transit Visa?
A: Normally, Transit Visas cannot be extended. However, exceptions
are made for extreme emergencies such as inclement weather,
strikes, or medical illness. Outside of these rare cases, the 3-day limit
is firm.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: What happens if I don't use my Transit Visa within the 15-
day window?
A: If you don't perform your journey within that period, the visa
becomes invalid. You cannot renew it; you would have to apply and
pay for a fresh Transit Visa.
---CHUNK---
[INDIA TRANSIT VISA: FAQ]
Q: Can I leave the airport to stay at a hotel for two nights on
this visa?
A: Yes! As long as your stay is under 3 days, a Transit Visa allows you
to leave the airport precincts. If your hotel stay or visit exceeds 3
days, you must obtain an appropriate category visa, such as a Tourist
Visa, before you arrive.

[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: What exactly does it mean to be a "Foreigner of Indian
Origin" (FIO)?
A: You are considered a Foreigner of Indian Origin if you meet one of
two criteria:
a. You have held an Indian passport at any time in the past.
b. You, or any of your parents, grandparents, or great-grandparents,
were born in and were permanent residents of India.
---CHUNK---
[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: Are there any countries that are excluded from this FIO
definition?
A: Yes. Even if you have Indian ancestry, you are generally not eligible
for this status if you (or the ancestors you are tracing through) have
ever been a citizen of Afghanistan, Bangladesh, China, Pakistan, or
Sri Lanka. The Government of India may also specify other countries
from time to time.
---CHUNK---
[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: Which visa is best for an FIO visiting India?
A: The most appropriate category is the Entry (X-1) Visa. While FIOs
can apply for other visas (like Tourist or Business) depending on their
specific purpose, the X-1 visa is specifically designed for people of
Indian origin and their families.
---CHUNK---
[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: Who else is eligible for the X-1 Entry Visa besides the FIO
themselves?
A: The X-1 Visa is also available to:
• The spouses and children of Indian Citizens, FIOs, or OCI
(Overseas Citizen of India) cardholders, even if those children were
born in a foreign country.
• People of Indian origin from former French territories,
specifically those born in Puducherry, Mahe, and Yanam.
---CHUNK---
[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: Can I use an X-1 Visa if I want to work or start a business
in India?
A: No. The X-1 Visa is primarily for visiting and staying with family. If
your purpose is professional, you should apply for an Employment or
Business Visa, even if you are of Indian origin.
---CHUNK---
[FOREIGNERS/PERSONS OF INDIAN ORIGIN (FIO)]
Q: What is the difference between an FIO and an OCI
cardholder?
A: "Foreigner of Indian Origin" is a general description of your
heritage. An OCI (Overseas Citizen of India) is a specific, lifelong
status you apply for that grants you a "lifelong visa" and many of the
same rights as Indian citizens (excluding voting and buying
agricultural land). Most FIOs prefer to get an OCI card because it
eliminates the need to apply for a visa every time they visit.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: Can foreigners visit any part of India they wish?
A: Generally, yes, but certain regions are classified as Protected Areas
or Restricted Areas. Foreigners are not normally allowed to visit these
zones unless they have extraordinary reasons and the proper permits.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: Which areas in India require a special permit (RAP/PAP)?
A: You will need a permit (Restricted Area Permit or Protected Area
Permit) to visit the following:
• Entire States/UTs: Arunachal Pradesh, Manipur, Mizoram,
Nagaland, and the Andaman & Nicobar Islands.
• Specific Parts of States: Parts of Himachal Pradesh, Jammu &
Kashmir, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand, and Sikkim (which is partly
Protected and partly Restricted).
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: How do I apply for a RAP or PAP?
A: The application process depends on where you are currently
located:
• Outside India: Apply through your nearest Indian Mission
(Embassy/Consulate) at least 30 days before your planned visit.
• Already in India: Apply to the jurisdictional FRRO (Foreigners
Regional Registration Office) at least 15 days in advance.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: If I have a permit, can I go anywhere within that restricted
area?
A: Not necessarily. A RAP/PAP grants you entry to the general region,
but you may still need additional permission from the specific officer
in charge of certain installations, national parks, or specific islands
within that area.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: What are the consequences of visiting these areas without
a permit?
A: Visiting a Restricted or Protected area without a valid permit is a
serious legal offense. Under the Immigration and Foreigners Act,
2025, strict action will be taken against anyone found in violation of
these security protocols.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: Are there any group requirements for these permits?
A: Yes, in many cases (especially for PAP), tourists are required to
travel in groups of at least two people and must be accompanied by
a registered travel agent. Independent travel to certain zones may be
restricted.
---CHUNK---
[RESTRICTED & PROTECTED AREA PERMITS]
Q: Is the list of restricted areas permanent?
A: No. The Government of India updates these locations from time to
time. Always check with the local FRRO or the Indian Embassy for the
most current list of notified places before you finalize your itinerary.
---CHUNK---
[IMMIGRATION FOR INDIAN CITIZENS]
Q: Do Indian citizens need to go through immigration when
traveling abroad?
A: Yes. Indian citizens are required to obtain immigration clearance
both when arriving in India and when departing for a foreign country.
---CHUNK---
[IMMIGRATION FOR INDIAN CITIZENS]
Q: What documents do I need to carry when leaving India?
A: To clear immigration at departure, you must carry:
• A valid Indian Passport.
• A valid Visa for your destination country.
• Any other specific clearances required for your travel (such as
OCI cards for family members or specific health declarations).
---CHUNK---
[IMMIGRATION FOR INDIAN CITIZENS]
Q: I used an e-Gate at the airport, but my passport wasn't
stamped. Is this a problem?
A: No, it’s perfectly normal! If you use the automated e-Gates (part
of the Fast Track Immigration program), the system processes your
clearance digitally. Your passport will not receive a physical ink stamp.
---CHUNK---
[IMMIGRATION FOR INDIAN CITIZENS]
Q: How can I check my travel history if my passport isn't

A: You can view your arrival and departure details online. Simply log
in to your Fast Track Immigration (FTI-TTP) profile on the official
website: ftittp.mha.gov.in.
Q5: What is the FTI-TTP program mentioned on the website?
A: It stands for the Fast Track Immigration – Trusted Traveller
Programme. It’s a government initiative that allows pre-verified
Indian nationals and OCI cardholders to use biometric e-gates for
faster, contactless immigration clearance.

[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: What is "Emigration Clearance" and who needs it?
A: Emigration Clearance is a legal requirement under the Emigration
Act, 1983. It is mandatory for Indian passport holders in the ECR
(Emigration Check Required) category who are traveling to specific
countries for employment.
---CHUNK---
[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: Which countries require me to get POE clearance before I
fly for work?
A: Currently, there are 18 countries where clearance is required for
ECR passport holders:
• Middle East: UAE, Saudi Arabia (KSA), Qatar, Oman, Kuwait,
Bahrain, Jordan, Yemen, Syria, Lebanon, Iraq, Iran.
• Africa: Libya, Sudan.
• Asia: Malaysia, Indonesia, Thailand, Afghanistan.
---CHUNK---
[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: Is it true that I cannot travel to Iraq for work?
A: Yes, currently emigration to Iraq is banned. Even for those with
ECNR passports, traveling to Iraq for work usually requires special
government clearance or is restricted due to security concerns.
---CHUNK---
[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: How do I know if my passport is "ECR" or "ECNR"?
A: Look at the last page of your passport.
• If it has an endorsement saying “Emigration Check Required,”
you are in the ECR category.
• If there is no such endorsement, or if it explicitly says "ECNR"
(or refers to the holder as being in a non-ECR category), you do
not need POE clearance for travel.
---CHUNK---
[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: I have an ECR passport, but I'm just going to the UAE for a
10-day holiday. Do I still need clearance?
A: No. Since October 1st, 2007, ECR passport holders traveling for
purposes other than employment (like tourism, visiting family, or
study) do not need POE clearance. You simply need your valid
passport, a valid visa, and a return ticket to show at the immigration
counter.
---CHUNK---
[PROTECTOR OF EMIGRANTS (POE) OFFICE DIRECTORY]
Q: Are there any professional groups that always need
clearance, regardless of their passport status?
A: Yes. Even if you have an ECNR passport, you must obtain POE
clearance if you fall into these specific categories and are traveling to
any of the 18 ECR countries:
• Nurses traveling for work.
• Seafarers traveling to join a vessel abroad.
• Anyone traveling to Iraq (due to the current restrictions).

Q: What is the e-Migrate system for seafarers?
A: The e-Migrate system is a mandatory digital platform launched by

protect Indian seafarers, particularly those recruited for foreign-

unlicensed Recruitment & Placement Services (RPS). By using e-

through a legitimate agency and has a valid contract.

system.

verify e-Migrate status before allowing seafarers to depart.

the adopted child must obtain exit permits to travel out of India.

Q: Does India allow dual citizenship?
A: No. India does not recognize the concept of dual nationality. The
moment an Indian national acquires foreign citizenship, they
immediately cease to be an Indian national.
---CHUNK---
[ACQUIRING FOREIGN NATIONALITY]
Q: Can a former Indian national continue to use their Indian
passport after getting a foreign one?
A: There is a strict 3-month grace period starting from the day of
acquisition of the new nationality (e.g., the date on the Naturalization
Certificate). Once that grace period expires, holding, acquiring, or
traveling on an Indian passport constitutes an offense under the
Indian Passport Act, 1967, and attracts severe penalties.
---CHUNK---
[ACQUIRING FOREIGN NATIONALITY]
Q: What must a person do if they acquire foreign citizenship
while they are staying in India?
A: If an individual acquires foreign nationality while residing in India,
they must take the following steps:
• Obtain the passport of their new nationality.
• Surrender their Indian passport, Indian Voter ID, Indian Ration
card, and all other relevant Indian nationality documents.
• Apply for appropriate visa services on the e-FRRO portal
(https://indianfrro.gov.in) within 30 days of acquiring the
foreign nationality.
---CHUNK---
[ACQUIRING FOREIGN NATIONALITY]

A: If an individual enters India on an Indian passport after having
already acquired foreign citizenship, they must immediately do the
following:
• Surrender their Indian passport, Indian Voter ID, Indian Ration
card, and all other related Indian nationality documents.
• Apply for visa services on the e-FRRO portal
(https://indianfrro.gov.in) within 30 days of their arrival in
India.

[PENALTIES AND LEGAL CONSEQUENCES]
Q: What actions constitute a violation under the Immigration
and Foreigners Act, 2025?
A: Legal or penal action may be taken against a foreigner for the
following violations:
• Overstaying beyond the visa validity or stay stipulation.
• Non-registration with the FRRO/FRO when required.
• Engaging in unauthorized activities that do not match the
specific visa category granted (e.g., working on a tourist visa).
• Non-filing of requisite forms (such as Form III for
accommodations).
• Any other contravention of provisions or orders made under the
Act.
---CHUNK---
[PENALTIES AND LEGAL CONSEQUENCES]
Q: What are the financial and legal penalties for these
offenses?
A: Violations of the Act carry significant consequences. Depending on
the nature of the offense, penalties include:
• Fines: Ranging from ₹10,000 to ₹3,00,000 for each individual
offense.
• Imprisonment: Serious violations may result in jail time as
prescribed under the various sections of the Act.
---CHUNK---
[PENALTIES AND LEGAL CONSEQUENCES]
Q: Are Indian citizens or businesses also liable for a
foreigner’s visa violations?
A: Yes. The Act holds Indian stakeholders accountable if they are
associated with the foreigner. This includes:
• Carriers (Airlines/Transport companies).
• Hoteliers and accommodation providers.
• Educational Institutions.
• Hospitals and medical facilities. If these entities are found
violating any provisions (such as failing to report a foreigner’s
stay or medical treatment), they are equally liable for fines and
legal action.
---CHUNK---
[PENALTIES AND LEGAL CONSEQUENCES]
Q: How does a visa violation affect a foreigner's future travel
to India?
A: Beyond immediate fines or jail time, violating Indian visa norms is
highly likely to result in a blacklisting or a negative record. This will
adversely affect the foreigner's chances of obtaining an Indian visa or
being granted entry into the country in the future.

Q: What is the difference between “Visa validity/Visa expiry date” and “Stay stipulation”?
A: Visa validity or Visa expiry date refers to the validity period during which the foreigner can stay in India on the strength of Visa subject to stay stipulations. Stay stipulations are the rules which determine the number of days a foreigner may be permitted to stay in India on the strength of Visa. Stay stipulations are of two kinds 1) Continuous stay in India from the date of last arrival. 2) Total Aggregate stay in India during the calendar year.
